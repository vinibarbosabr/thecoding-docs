# Developer Relations (DevRel)

Developer relations (DevRel) work is one of the most important roles for open-source software, infrastructure, protocols, and SaaS.
There will hardly be a successful blockchain and web3 project without solid DevRel work.

I help teams create practical, technically accurate developer experiences that reduce friction, improve onboarding, and make builders more likely to ship with your product (and continue supporting it over time).

The focus is always the same: clarity, real usage, and useful outcomes for developers.

<Tip>
  Available for education sprints, workshops & talks, onboarding improvements, or ongoing part-time DevRel support.
</Tip>

## What I actually do

I combine technical writing, documentation engineering, hands-on product understanding, and real community experience to support developers.

Typical work includes:

- Organizing and running workshops and hackathons (remote or IRL)
- Giving technical talks at conferences, universities, meetups, and webinars
- Creating high-quality technical tutorials, guides, and educational content
- Improving the developer onboarding path (from first contact to successful integration)
- Identifying and fixing friction points in docs, examples, and flows
- Supporting developers through clear explanations and practical feedback
- Bridging the gap between product, documentation, and external communication
- Ambassador-style community activation and regional developer outreach

I read the code, test the flows, and work directly with the people who build.

## Engagement models explained

### 1. Technical Education Sprint
Focused creation of high-quality tutorials and educational material that helps developers understand and use your product correctly.

### 2. Workshops, Talks & Events
Preparation and delivery of technical talks, hands-on workshops, and support for hackathons or IRL developer events. Can be remote or in-person (Brazil and selected locations).

### 3. Onboarding & DX Sprint
Deep look at the critical path a new developer takes, followed by concrete improvements in documentation, examples, and supporting content.

### 4. DevRel Partnership
Ongoing part-time support focused on education, onboarding health, and light community activation as the product evolves.

## How I work

<Steps>
  <Step title="Briefing / Discovery">
    We clarify goals, audience, format (content, workshop, talk, ongoing support), and constraints.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Research & Preparation">
    I study the product, existing materials, and developer flows. For talks and workshops I prepare clear, practical sessions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Delivery">
    Content is written and published, workshops/talks are delivered, or documentation improvements are opened as pull requests.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Feedback & Follow-up">
    One structured revision or feedback round is included for content and materials. Event follow-up content can be scoped separately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Summary & Next Steps">
    I provide a short summary of what was delivered and, when relevant, recommendations for continued developer experience work.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Most content and onboarding sprints take 2–4 weeks. Workshops and talks are scheduled according to the event calendar.
